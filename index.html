Luiz P. Gressmann

B.Sc. Physics student @ Goethe University Frankfurt

I'm a physics student interested in theoretical astrophysics, quantitative finance, factor investing, and computational methods for data-driven modelling. I'm particularly interested in compact objects such as neutron stars and intend to pursue research in this area as part of my bachelor's thesis.

Alongside my studies, I work on personal projects in computational physics and quantitative finance. One of my projects investigates how different long-only portfolio allocation strategies behave across developed-market equity factors. I compare simple approaches, such as equal weighting, with more sophisticated optimization methods, including OAS-shrinkage global minimum-variance (GMV) portfolios, within a historical walk-forward framework. The analysis focuses on estimation uncertainty, turnover costs, and the sensitivity of optimization results to different rolling windows, rebalancing schedules, and portfolio constraints. More details can be found here.

Currently, I am investigating how retroactive revisions to historical Fama-French factor data affect the performance and evaluation of factor-based portfolio strategies. The project is inspired by the work of Akey, Robertson, and Simutin, particularly their paper “Noisy Factors? The Retroactive Impact of Methodological Changes on the Fama-French Factors” . More details can be found here.
